How to Create a Professional CV

A professional curriculum vitae (CV) is a clear summary of your work experience, education, skills and qualifications. It helps employers understand your background and decide whether your experience matches a job opening. A well-organised CV should be easy to read, relevant to the position and accurate. FreeCV Maker helps you organise your information, choose a layout, preview your CV and save it as a PDF without creating an account.

What Should a Professional CV Include?

The sections in a CV depend on your career stage and the job requirements. Most job seekers can start with the following information:

  • Contact information: Your full name, professional email address, phone number and current city or country.
  • Professional summary: A short introduction explaining your experience, strengths and career focus.
  • Work experience: Relevant positions, employers, dates and responsibilities or achievements.
  • Education: Degrees, diplomas, professional qualifications and relevant training.
  • Skills: Job-related technical skills, software, tools and transferable abilities.
  • Languages: Languages you can use professionally and your honest proficiency level.
  • Certifications: Relevant courses, licences and professional certificates.

You do not need to include every possible section. Choose information that helps demonstrate your suitability for the particular role.

How to Write a Strong Professional Summary

Your professional summary is usually one of the first sections an employer reads. Keep it concise and specific. Mention your professional area, relevant experience, strongest skills and the kind of contribution you can make.

For example, an administrative professional might describe experience coordinating office activities, maintaining records, supporting teams and using relevant office software. A technical professional could highlight their specialist tools, project experience and technical responsibilities.

Avoid generic statements such as "I am hardworking and looking for a challenging opportunity" unless you support them with meaningful professional details. Use clear language and adapt your summary to the job description.

How to Describe Work Experience and Achievements

List your work experience in reverse chronological order, starting with your most recent position. For each role, provide your job title, employer, location and employment dates.

Describe your main responsibilities using concise statements. Where possible, include measurable achievements such as the number of projects completed, customers supported, reports prepared, deadlines achieved or processes improved. Only use figures that are accurate and that you can explain during an interview.

Use action words such as managed, prepared, designed, coordinated, analysed, maintained, developed and supported when they accurately describe your work.

How to Make Your CV More ATS-Friendly

Many employers use applicant tracking systems (ATS) to organise and review job applications. A readable CV can make it easier for both software and recruiters to identify relevant information. No particular format guarantees that a CV will pass every ATS, because systems and employer settings differ.

  • Use clear section headings such as Work Experience, Education and Skills.
  • Choose a simple layout when applying through online recruitment systems.
  • Use standard job titles and relevant terminology from the vacancy when it truthfully describes your experience.
  • Avoid unnecessary graphics, complex tables and decorative elements in an ATS-focused CV.
  • Use consistent dates, spelling and formatting throughout the document.
  • Check the exported PDF to make sure the text is readable and the layout is correct.

Helpful tip: The Professional ATS template in FreeCV Maker provides a straightforward single-column starting layout. Always check the employer's application instructions, because some employers request a specific file type or format.

CV Advice for Fresh Graduates and Job Seekers with No Experience

If you are a recent graduate or applying for your first job, you can still create a useful CV without full-time work experience. Focus on your education, relevant coursework, academic projects, internships, volunteering, practical training and transferable skills.

Explain what you did, which tools or knowledge you used and what you learned. Include part-time work or other responsibilities when they demonstrate skills relevant to the role. Do not invent employment history or qualifications.

For more detailed advice, read our CV format guide for freshers and guide to writing a CV with no work experience.

CV Advice for Experienced Professionals

Experienced candidates should prioritise relevant career achievements, specialist knowledge, leadership responsibilities and professional development. You do not need to describe every task from every previous job. Give more space to recent roles and experience that closely matches the position you are applying for.

Keep your job titles, employment dates and qualifications accurate. Review older information and remove details that no longer help demonstrate your suitability.

How to Prepare a CV for Jobs in Saudi Arabia

When applying for jobs in Saudi Arabia, follow the employer's application instructions and the conventions used in your industry. Make your professional title, relevant experience, qualifications and contact details easy to find. If you have Saudi work experience, describe your actual responsibilities and projects clearly.

Include a professional photo only when it is appropriate for the role and requested or accepted by the employer. Do not include sensitive personal information unless it is specifically required and appropriate.

Common CV Mistakes to Avoid

  • Typos, grammar mistakes and inconsistent formatting.
  • Using the same generic CV for every application without considering the job description.
  • Including inaccurate dates, qualifications or achievements.
  • Writing long paragraphs that make important information difficult to find.
  • Adding unrelated personal details or outdated information.
  • Using an unprofessional email address.
  • Sending a PDF without checking its appearance and readability.

Before applying, read the CV from beginning to end and check that your contact details are correct. Ask someone you trust to review it if possible. You can also use our guide to common CV mistakes.
