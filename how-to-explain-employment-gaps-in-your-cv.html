How to Explain Employment Gaps in Your CV in 2026

Last updated: November 1, 2026

An employment gap is a period when you were not working in a formal job. It can happen for many reasons, including personal responsibilities, further education, health and family needs, relocation, redundancy or difficulty finding suitable employment.

If your CV contains a gap between jobs, you may worry about how employers will view it. The good news is that an employment gap does not automatically make you an unsuitable candidate.

What matters is presenting your work history honestly, explaining relevant periods clearly when necessary and showing that your skills and experience match the position.

This guide explains how to handle employment gaps in your CV, what to say during interviews and how to present your professional background confidently.

1. What Is an Employment Gap?

An employment gap is a period between jobs or a period when you were not employed. For example, if your previous job ended in January 2023 and your next job started in October 2023, there is a gap between those employment dates.

Employment gaps can be short or long. They may happen at different stages of a person's career.

Common reasons include:

  • Searching for a new job.
  • Taking care of family responsibilities.
  • Relocating to another city or country.
  • Studying or completing professional training.
  • Taking a personal break.
  • Recovering from a health issue.
  • Being affected by redundancy or company closure.
  • Starting a business or working independently.
  • Taking time to improve professional skills.

These circumstances are part of many people's careers. You do not need to assume that an employment gap will prevent you from finding another job.

2. Should You Include Employment Gaps in Your CV?

You should always provide accurate employment dates and avoid creating a misleading work history.

However, you do not necessarily need to explain every short break directly in your CV. The amount of detail depends on the length of the gap, your career history and the requirements of the position.

For example:

  • A short gap between two jobs may not need a separate explanation.
  • A longer gap may benefit from a brief, professional explanation.
  • A period spent studying or completing training may be included if it is relevant.
  • Freelance or independent work can be included if it genuinely took place.
Important: Never change employment dates or invent a job to hide a gap. Accurate information helps maintain your professional credibility.

3. Identify the Reason for Your Employment Gap

Before updating your CV, identify the actual reason for the break. You do not need to share private personal details, but you should be prepared to give a clear and professional explanation if an employer asks.

Think about the following:

  • When did the employment gap begin and end?
  • What was the main reason for the break?
  • Did you complete any training or learning during that time?
  • Did you work on personal projects or independent assignments?
  • Are you now available and ready to work?

Preparing these points can help you answer questions without becoming uncomfortable or giving unnecessary personal information.

4. How to Explain Different Types of Employment Gaps

A. Employment Gap Due to Family Responsibilities

Some people take time away from employment to care for children, parents or other family members.

You can explain this period briefly without sharing private family information.

Example CV explanation:

Career Break – Family Responsibilities

January 2024 – August 2024

Took a planned career break to manage family responsibilities. Now available and ready to return to professional employment.

B. Employment Gap Due to Further Education

If you used the time to study, complete a qualification or improve your professional knowledge, include the relevant details.

Example CV explanation:

Professional Development

March 2023 – December 2023

Focused on professional development and completed relevant training to strengthen technical and professional skills.

C. Employment Gap Due to Relocation

Moving to another country or city can create a temporary break in employment. You can explain that you were relocating and preparing to continue your career in a new location.

Example CV explanation:

Career Break – Relocation

June 2023 – November 2023

Relocated to a new location and completed the arrangements required to resume professional employment.

D. Employment Gap Due to Job Searching

Sometimes finding a suitable position takes longer than expected. You do not need to provide a lengthy explanation.

Example interview explanation:

"After my previous position ended, I spent time looking for a suitable opportunity that matched my professional experience and career goals. I am now ready to take on a new role."

E. Employment Gap Due to Redundancy or Company Closure

If your previous position ended because of redundancy or a company closure, explain the situation briefly and focus on your next career opportunity.

Example interview explanation:

"My previous position ended following organisational changes at the company. Since then, I have been looking for an opportunity where I can use my experience and contribute to a new team."

F. Employment Gap Due to Personal Reasons

You may have taken a break for personal reasons. You are not required to provide unnecessary private details in your CV.

Example CV explanation:

Career Break – Personal Reasons

February 2024 – July 2024

Took a personal career break and am now prepared to return to full-time professional employment.

5. How to Explain an Employment Gap During an Interview

An interviewer may ask why you were not working during a particular period. Prepare a short answer that is honest, calm and focused on your readiness for the role.

A useful approach is:

  1. Briefly explain the reason for the gap.
  2. Avoid unnecessary personal information.
  3. Mention any relevant learning or activities, if applicable.
  4. Explain that you are ready to move forward professionally.
Example answer:

"I had a period away from employment due to personal responsibilities. During that time, I managed those commitments and considered my next career step. Those circumstances are now settled, and I am ready to focus on my professional responsibilities again."

Adapt the answer to your actual circumstances. Do not use an explanation that is untrue simply because it sounds more attractive.

6. Highlight Skills and Activities During Your Career Break

An employment gap does not always mean you stopped learning or developing. You may have completed activities that demonstrate your continued interest in your profession.

Consider including relevant activities such as:

  • Online courses and professional training.
  • Industry certifications.
  • Freelance projects or independent work.
  • Volunteer activities.
  • Personal projects and practical learning.
  • Professional development and software practice.

Only include activities you genuinely completed. If you mention a qualification or project, be prepared to discuss it during the interview.

7. Choose a CV Format That Presents Your Experience Clearly

The right CV format can help employers understand your background without unnecessary confusion.

Reverse Chronological Format

This format lists your most recent employment first. It is commonly used and helps recruiters understand your career progression.

Skills-Based or Functional Format

This format places more emphasis on relevant skills and abilities. It may help some candidates explain transferable skills, career changes or varied experience.

However, it should still include accurate employment history and dates. Do not use a skills-based format to conceal important information.

Combination Format

A combination CV highlights professional skills and employment experience. It can be useful for candidates who want to demonstrate both technical abilities and career history.

Choose the format that makes your qualifications and experience easiest to understand.

8. How to Handle Employment Gaps in an ATS-Friendly CV

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to organise applications and identify relevant information.

To make your CV easier to read:

  • Use clear job titles and employer names.
  • Provide accurate employment dates.
  • Use consistent date formatting.
  • Include relevant skills and qualifications.
  • Use standard section headings such as Work Experience and Education.
  • Avoid complicated layouts that make information difficult to read.
  • Keep explanations short and professional.

Do not assume that an ATS will automatically reject a CV because it contains an employment gap. Employer requirements and recruitment processes vary.

9. Employment Gaps for Freshers and Career Changers

Employment gaps are not limited to experienced professionals. Fresh graduates and people changing careers may also have periods without formal employment.

If you are a fresher, focus on your education, projects, internships, practical skills and relevant training.

If you are changing careers, highlight transferable skills and activities that demonstrate your preparation for the new field.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid When Explaining Employment Gaps

Try to avoid these mistakes when preparing your CV or answering interview questions:

  • Changing employment dates to hide a gap.
  • Inventing jobs, freelance work or qualifications.
  • Providing unnecessary private information.
  • Giving long and confusing explanations.
  • Blaming previous employers or colleagues.
  • Sounding defensive when discussing your career break.
  • Leaving relevant training or genuine activities out of your CV.
  • Using inconsistent employment dates.
  • Failing to explain a significant gap when directly asked.
  • Forgetting to highlight your current skills and readiness for work.

Your aim should be to present an accurate and professional career history, not to make every period look identical.

11. Employment Gap Explanation Checklist

Before submitting your CV, review this checklist:

  • Check that all employment dates are accurate.
  • Identify any significant gaps in your work history.
  • Prepare a short and honest explanation if needed.
  • Include relevant training and professional development.
  • Highlight genuine projects or independent work.
  • Use a clear and consistent CV format.
  • Check spelling, grammar and date formatting.
  • Make sure your current skills match the job requirements.
  • Practise answering employment-gap questions before interviews.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Will an employment gap affect my chances of getting a job?

It may lead an employer to ask questions, but an employment gap does not automatically disqualify you. Your qualifications, experience, skills and suitability for the role remain important.

2. Should I explain a short employment gap in my CV?

Not necessarily. A short gap between jobs may not require a separate explanation. Always provide accurate employment dates.

3. How do I explain a two-year employment gap?

Give a brief and honest explanation of the reason for the break. If you completed relevant training, projects or other activities, mention them where appropriate.

4. Should I include personal or medical details?

You generally do not need to disclose unnecessary private details in your CV. A brief description such as "personal career break" may be sufficient, depending on the circumstances.

5. Can I include freelance work during an employment gap?

Yes, if you genuinely completed freelance or independent work during that period. Describe the work accurately and include relevant dates and responsibilities.

6. What if I was unemployed and did not complete any training?

You can simply explain that you were looking for a suitable opportunity. You do not need to invent activities or qualifications to fill the period.

7. Should I use a functional CV to hide employment gaps?

No. A skills-based format can highlight relevant abilities, but your employment history should remain accurate and understandable.

Conclusion

Employment gaps are a normal part of many professional journeys. They can happen because of family responsibilities, education, relocation, personal circumstances or changes in the job market.

The best approach is to be honest, keep explanations professional and focus on the experience and skills you can offer. Do not let a career break prevent you from applying for suitable opportunities.

Prepare a clear CV that accurately represents your professional background and demonstrates why you are a suitable candidate for the position.
