Common CV Mistakes to Avoid in 2026

Last updated: November 1, 2026

A CV is often one of the first things an employer reviews when considering a job application. A well-prepared CV can make your experience, qualifications and skills easier to understand. However, even experienced professionals can make mistakes that weaken their applications.

Some problems are related to formatting, while others involve unclear work experience, irrelevant information, spelling errors or missing details. These mistakes can make it harder for recruiters to identify whether a candidate is suitable for a position.

This guide explains common CV mistakes to avoid in 2026 and provides practical suggestions for preparing a clear, professional and effective CV.

1. Using an Unprofessional CV Format

One common mistake is choosing a CV design that looks attractive but is difficult to read. Excessive colours, decorative graphics, complicated layouts and unusual fonts can distract from important information.

A professional CV should have a clear structure, consistent formatting and readable section headings.

How to improve it:

  • Choose a clean and professional CV template.
  • Use a readable font such as Arial, Calibri or another standard font.
  • Keep headings and spacing consistent.
  • Avoid unnecessary graphics and decorative elements.
  • Make sure your contact information is easy to find.

2. Including Too Much Unnecessary Information

Your CV should focus on information that is relevant to the position you are applying for. Including every personal detail, unrelated activity or outdated qualification can make the document unnecessarily long.

Recruiters need to identify your professional background and relevant abilities without searching through unrelated information.

How to improve it:

  • Include relevant education and professional experience.
  • Remove unrelated personal information.
  • Focus on skills that match the vacancy.
  • Keep older experience concise when it is less relevant.
  • Use clear and direct language.

3. Writing a Weak Professional Summary

The professional summary introduces your background and helps employers understand your experience. A vague summary that only uses general words such as hardworking, motivated or dedicated does not explain what you can actually offer.

Instead, describe your profession, experience, main areas of expertise and relevant technical skills.

Weak example:

I am a hardworking and responsible person looking for a good opportunity in a reputed company.

Improved example:

Experienced Architectural Draftsman with 14 years of experience in architectural drafting and construction documentation. Skilled in AutoCAD and Revit Architecture, with experience supporting project teams and preparing technical drawings.

The improved example provides more useful information about the candidate's professional background.

4. Listing Job Duties Without Explaining Your Work

Another common mistake is describing previous employment using only general responsibilities. Employers want to understand the type of work you performed and how your experience relates to their requirements.

Instead of using vague statements, describe your actual responsibilities, tools, project involvement and achievements where applicable.

Weak example:

Responsible for preparing drawings and doing office work.

Improved example:

Prepared and updated architectural construction drawings using AutoCAD, coordinated drawing revisions with project teams and maintained technical documentation.

Only include responsibilities and achievements that accurately reflect your experience.

5. Making Spelling and Grammar Mistakes

Spelling errors, incorrect grammar and inconsistent punctuation can make a CV appear unfinished. Even a candidate with strong professional experience may create a poor impression if the document contains repeated writing mistakes.

How to improve it:

  • Check spelling and grammar before submitting.
  • Use consistent punctuation and capitalisation.
  • Read the complete CV slowly.
  • Ask someone to review it if possible.
  • Check company names, job titles and employment dates carefully.

6. Using the Same CV for Every Job Application

Sending exactly the same CV to every employer may reduce its relevance to individual vacancies. Different positions can require different technical abilities, qualifications and experience.

A CV for an Architectural Draftsman position may need to highlight AutoCAD, Revit Architecture and construction drawings. A project coordination position may require more emphasis on documentation, communication and coordination experience.

How to improve it:

  • Read the job advertisement carefully.
  • Identify the main skills required.
  • Highlight relevant experience.
  • Use accurate keywords from the job description where appropriate.
  • Keep your employment history and qualifications truthful.

7. Ignoring Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)

Some employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to help organize applications and identify relevant information. A complicated CV layout or unclear section headings may make information harder for some systems to process.

How to improve it:

  • Use a simple, readable layout.
  • Choose standard section headings such as Work Experience, Education and Skills.
  • Include relevant job-related keywords naturally.
  • Avoid unnecessary tables, text boxes and graphics when compatibility is important.
  • Follow the employer's requested file format.

Remember that ATS systems differ. No particular CV format can guarantee a specific ranking, interview or successful application.

8. Providing Incorrect Contact Information

Incorrect telephone numbers, outdated email addresses and incomplete contact details can prevent employers from reaching you.

Before sending your CV, check that your contact information is accurate and professional.

Include:

  • Your full professional name.
  • A regularly checked email address.
  • Your correct telephone number, including country code where relevant.
  • Your current city and country.
  • Your LinkedIn profile, if relevant and up to date.

Avoid including unnecessary private information that is not required for the application.

9. Leaving Employment Dates and Job Titles Unclear

Employers may find it difficult to understand your career history if employment dates, job titles or company names are missing or inconsistent.

Use a consistent format for each employment entry.

Example:

Senior Architectural CAD Operator
ABC Engineering Consultancy
January 2023 – Present

Key Responsibilities:

  • Prepared architectural drawings and technical documentation.
  • Updated drawings based on project revisions.
  • Supported coordination with other project disciplines.

Use accurate employment dates and do not change job titles to make your experience appear more senior than it was.

10. Making Your CV Too Long or Too Short

A CV should contain enough information to explain your professional background without unnecessary repetition.

For many applicants, one or two pages may be sufficient. However, experienced professionals with extensive relevant project experience may reasonably need additional space.

There is no single page limit that applies to every profession or employer. Follow the application instructions and prioritize relevant information.

11. Including Skills You Cannot Demonstrate

Adding software, technical skills or professional abilities that you do not actually possess can create problems during interviews or employment assessments.

For example, listing advanced Revit skills when you have only basic experience may lead to questions that you cannot answer confidently.

How to improve it:

  • List skills that you genuinely have.
  • Describe your proficiency accurately.
  • Separate technical skills from general professional skills.
  • Be prepared to explain how you used each important skill.
  • Remove outdated or irrelevant skills.

12. Forgetting to Update Your CV

An outdated CV may not reflect your current employment, qualifications, software knowledge or professional achievements.

Update your CV whenever you gain important experience, complete relevant training, change jobs or develop new professional skills.

Review your contact details and employment dates before every important application.

13. Sending the Wrong File Format

Submitting a CV in an unsuitable file format can cause problems for employers. Some organizations request PDF files, while others specifically ask for Word documents.

How to improve it:

  • Follow the employer's application instructions.
  • Use PDF when a fixed-layout document is requested or accepted.
  • Use Word when the employer specifically requests a DOC or DOCX file.
  • Open your saved file to check that the formatting is correct.
  • Choose a clear file name, such as Firstname-Lastname-CV.pdf.

14. Forgetting to Check the Final CV

Even after preparing a good CV, small errors can remain in the final document. Text may be cut off, page breaks may look unusual or information may be missing from the saved file.

Always review the final PDF or Word document before submitting it.

Final CV Review Checklist

Use this checklist before sending your next job application:

  • My name and contact information are correct.
  • My professional summary clearly explains my background.
  • My CV has a clean and readable layout.
  • My employment dates and job titles are accurate.
  • My work experience is relevant to the vacancy.
  • My skills reflect my actual abilities.
  • My education and certifications are correctly listed.
  • I have checked spelling and grammar.
  • I have removed unnecessary personal information.
  • I have included relevant keywords naturally.
  • I have followed the employer's file-format instructions.
  • I have opened and checked the final saved document.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most common CV mistake?

There is no single mistake that applies to every applicant. However, unclear work experience, poor formatting, irrelevant information and spelling errors are common problems that can weaken a CV.

Can a poorly formatted CV affect an application?

Yes. A difficult-to-read layout can make it harder for recruiters to find important information. Some complicated formats may also create compatibility issues with certain ATS platforms.

How many pages should a professional CV have?

The appropriate length depends on your experience, profession and the employer's requirements. One or two pages may be enough for many applicants, while some experienced professionals need more space to explain relevant work.

Should I include a photograph in my CV?

It depends on the country, industry and employer. Follow the job advertisement and local recruitment expectations. Do not include a photograph when the employer requests that applicants avoid one.

Should I include all my previous jobs?

Include relevant employment history and provide enough information to explain your career. Older or unrelated positions can be described more briefly when appropriate.

Can I use the same CV for different jobs?

You can use the same basic CV as a starting point, but it is usually better to adjust the professional summary, skills and relevant experience to match each vacancy.

How often should I update my CV?

Review your CV regularly and update it whenever you gain relevant experience, qualifications, certifications or professional skills. Check it again before submitting each application.

Conclusion

A professional CV does not need complicated designs or unnecessary information. It needs to present your experience, qualifications and skills clearly and accurately.

By avoiding common CV mistakes, checking your information and adapting your application to each vacancy, you can prepare a document that is easier for employers to review.

Take time to review your CV before submitting it. Small improvements in clarity, accuracy and presentation can make your application more professional.
