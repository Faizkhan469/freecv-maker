How to Write a Professional CV in 2026: Complete Guide

Last updated: October 31, 2026

A professional CV helps employers understand your qualifications, work experience and skills. A well-organized CV makes important information easier to find and helps you present your professional background clearly.

This guide explains how to prepare a professional CV step by step, including practical examples and tips for creating an ATS-friendly document.

1. Choose a Clear and Professional CV Format

Your CV should have a simple layout that makes information easy to read. Choose a format that suits your experience and the type of job you are applying for.

  • Chronological CV: Lists your most recent employment first. It is useful for people with a consistent work history.
  • Functional CV: Gives more attention to skills and qualifications. It may suit people changing careers or with employment gaps.
  • Combination CV: Highlights both relevant skills and employment history.

For many experienced professionals, a reverse chronological format is a practical choice because employers can quickly see recent experience.

2. Add Your Personal Information

Start with your essential contact details so employers can identify you and contact you easily.

  • Full name
  • Professional job title
  • Email address
  • Phone number with country code
  • City and country
  • LinkedIn profile, if relevant

Use a professional email address. Avoid including unnecessary personal details such as identification numbers, passport information or family information.

3. Write a Strong Professional Summary

A professional summary is a short introduction near the beginning of your CV. It should describe your experience, main skills and professional strengths.

Keep it relevant to the position you want. Avoid generic statements that could describe almost any applicant.

Example of a professional summary

Experienced administrative professional with a background in office coordination, document management and customer service. Skilled in Microsoft Office, communication and organizing daily administrative tasks. Focused on accuracy, teamwork and efficient office operations.

Use this as a writing example, not as a statement to copy if it does not reflect your actual experience.

4. Present Your Work Experience Clearly

List your work experience starting with your most recent position. For each job, include:

  • Job title
  • Company name
  • Location
  • Employment dates
  • Main responsibilities and achievements

Example

Office Administrator
ABC Company – Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
January 2022 – September 2026
  • Managed daily office records and administrative documents.
  • Coordinated appointments and internal communications.
  • Prepared reports and maintained organized filing systems.

Use action words such as managed, prepared, coordinated, developed, supported and completed. Include measurable results when you can support them with accurate information.

5. Include Your Education and Qualifications

List your relevant education, starting with your highest or most recent qualification.

Include the qualification name, institution and completion year. You may also include relevant coursework or academic achievements where useful.

6. Highlight Relevant Skills

Choose skills that match the job description and that you genuinely possess.

Depending on your profession, these may include:

  • Technical and software skills
  • Communication
  • Project coordination
  • Problem-solving
  • Time management
  • Teamwork

Be specific where possible. For example, list software applications by name rather than writing only "computer skills".

7. Add Languages and Certifications

List the languages you can use professionally and describe your proficiency honestly. Include relevant certificates, professional training and the issuing organization.

8. Make Your CV ATS-Friendly

Many employers use Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS) to organize and review applications. No particular CV layout guarantees that a system will read or rank your application correctly, but these practices can improve clarity and compatibility:

  • Use clear section headings such as Work Experience, Education and Skills.
  • Use a readable font and consistent formatting.
  • Include relevant keywords from the job description when they accurately describe your experience.
  • Avoid unnecessary graphics, complex tables and decorative elements.
  • Use standard employment dates and job titles.
  • Check that the downloaded PDF contains selectable, readable text.

A simple single-column layout is often a sensible starting point for applications where ATS compatibility is important.

9. Common CV Mistakes to Avoid

  • Spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • Incorrect contact details.
  • Long paragraphs that are difficult to scan.
  • Unrelated information that does not support your application.
  • Exaggerated or inaccurate qualifications and achievements.
  • The same generic CV for every job application.
  • Unprofessional email addresses.

10. Review Your CV Before Sending It

Before applying for a job, review your CV carefully. Confirm that dates, job titles, qualifications and contact details are correct. Check the PDF on a computer or phone to ensure the formatting is readable and no information is missing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should a professional CV be?

There is no single correct length for every profession. A concise one- or two-page CV is often suitable, while senior professionals or applicants with extensive relevant experience may need more space.

Should I customize my CV for each job?

Yes. Highlight the experience and skills most relevant to each position while keeping all information accurate.

Should I save my CV as a PDF?

PDF is commonly accepted because it helps preserve document formatting. However, always follow the employer's requested file format.

Can a free CV builder help me?

A CV builder can help organize your information into a structured document. You should still review the content for accuracy and tailor it to the job.
