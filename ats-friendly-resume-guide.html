How to Create an ATS-Friendly Resume in 2026

Last updated: October 31, 2026

An ATS-friendly resume is designed to make your professional information easier for Applicant Tracking Systems and recruiters to read. Many employers use these systems to organize job applications and search for relevant information.

This guide explains practical ways to prepare a clear, readable resume, choose suitable formatting and include relevant keywords without compromising accuracy.

1. Understand What an Applicant Tracking System Does

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software that employers use to manage recruitment. Depending on the system and employer settings, it may help collect applications, organize candidate information and support searches or filtering.

Different ATS platforms work in different ways. There is no universal format or guaranteed method that ensures every system will interpret a resume perfectly.

2. Choose a Simple Resume Layout

A clear layout helps both software and people find important information.

  • Use a simple single-column layout as a sensible starting point.
  • Keep section headings clear and familiar.
  • Use consistent spacing and readable fonts.
  • Keep dates and job titles in predictable positions.
  • Avoid unnecessary decorative elements.

Common section headings include Professional Summary, Work Experience, Education, Skills, Certifications and Languages.

3. Use Standard Fonts and Formatting

Choose a readable font such as Arial, Calibri or another commonly supported typeface. Use a consistent font size and make headings easy to distinguish.

Avoid placing essential information only inside images, graphics, icons or complicated text boxes. Some systems may not extract that information reliably.

4. Include Relevant Keywords from the Job Description

Read the job advertisement carefully and identify the qualifications, tools, software and skills that genuinely match your background.

For example, an architecture drafting position may mention:

  • AutoCAD
  • Revit Architecture
  • Architectural Drafting
  • Construction Drawings
  • Technical Documentation
  • Multidisciplinary Coordination

If you have these skills, include the relevant terms naturally in your summary, work experience or skills section. Never add skills or qualifications that you do not possess.

5. Write a Relevant Professional Summary

Start your resume with a concise professional summary that describes your experience, specialization and important strengths.

Example

Professional Summary

Architectural Draftsman with experience in preparing architectural drawings, construction documentation and coordinating design information. Skilled in AutoCAD and Revit Architecture, with a focus on drawing accuracy, documentation quality and project coordination.

This is an example only. Adapt it to your actual experience, software knowledge and target position.

6. Present Work Experience in Reverse Chronological Order

Usually, list your latest job first. Include your job title, employer, location, employment dates and relevant responsibilities.

Example

Architectural Draftsman
ABC Engineering Consultancy – Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
January 2023 – September 2026
  • Prepared architectural drawings using AutoCAD.
  • Updated construction documentation based on approved design changes.
  • Coordinated drawing information with relevant project teams.

Use accurate job titles and describe work you actually performed. Where possible, explain achievements with measurable results that you can verify.

7. Organize Your Skills Section

Use a clear skills section with relevant technical and professional abilities.

For example, a technical professional might separate skills into:

  • Software: AutoCAD, Revit, Microsoft Office.
  • Technical: Drawing preparation, documentation and coordination.
  • Professional: Communication, teamwork and time management.

Only list software and skills you can confidently discuss or demonstrate.

8. Include Education and Certifications

Clearly state your education, qualifications, institutions and completion years. Add relevant professional certificates and training where appropriate.

Make sure the names of qualifications and issuing organizations are accurate.

9. Choose the Correct File Format

PDF is commonly accepted and helps preserve formatting. However, some employers specifically request Microsoft Word documents or another format.

Always follow the application instructions. Before submitting a PDF, check that its text is selectable and readable rather than being only a scanned image.

10. Test Your Resume Before Applying

Open your final document and review it carefully. You can also copy the PDF text into a plain-text editor to check whether the reading order makes sense.

  • Check that your name and contact details are readable.
  • Confirm that headings appear in the correct order.
  • Check employment dates and job titles.
  • Look for missing or duplicated text.
  • Review spelling and grammar.
  • Confirm that the document matches the employer's requested format.
Important: An ATS-friendly format does not guarantee an interview, selection or a particular ATS score. Employers consider many factors, including qualifications, experience and job requirements.

Common ATS Resume Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using complicated layouts without checking text extraction.
  • Adding irrelevant keywords just to match a job advertisement.
  • Using graphics for important contact or qualification details.
  • Submitting a scanned document when readable text is required.
  • Including inaccurate employment dates or qualifications.
  • Ignoring the employer's file-format instructions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best resume format for ATS?

A simple, clearly structured resume with standard headings and readable text is a sensible starting point. However, no layout is guaranteed to work identically with every ATS.

Should I use keywords in my resume?

Yes. Include relevant words from the job description when they accurately describe your experience, qualifications and skills.

Are tables and columns bad for ATS?

Some systems may have difficulty interpreting complex layouts. A straightforward single-column design can reduce potential reading-order problems.

Is PDF or Word better for ATS?

Both may be accepted, depending on the employer and ATS. Follow the employer's instructions rather than assuming one format is always better.

Can I create an ATS-friendly resume for free?

Yes. You can use a free CV builder to organize your details, select a simple template and save your resume as a PDF. Always review the finished document before applying.
