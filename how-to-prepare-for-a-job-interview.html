How to Prepare for a Job Interview in 2026

Last updated: November 1, 2026

Preparing for a job interview is an important step towards getting a new job. Your CV may help you receive an interview invitation, but the interview gives you an opportunity to explain your experience, demonstrate your skills and show an employer why you are suitable for the position.

Whether you are applying for your first job or looking for a more senior position, proper preparation can help you feel more confident and communicate your abilities clearly.

This guide explains how to prepare for a job interview, answer common questions, discuss your experience and make a professional impression.

1. Research the Company Before the Interview

Start by learning about the company that invited you for an interview. Understanding its work and business activities can help you give more relevant answers.

Research the following information:

  • The company's main business activities and services.
  • Its products, projects or clients, where publicly available.
  • The industry in which it operates.
  • The location and nature of the position.
  • Recent company developments or publicly available news.
  • The company's website and professional social media profiles.
Practical tip: Prepare two or three facts about the company that you can naturally mention during the interview.

2. Understand the Job Description

Read the job advertisement carefully. Identify the main responsibilities, qualifications, software, technical abilities and experience requested by the employer.

Compare these requirements with your own background. Think about examples from your previous work, education or projects that demonstrate your suitability.

For example, if the position requires AutoCAD and Revit experience, prepare to explain:

  • How many years you have worked with each application.
  • What types of drawings or models you have prepared.
  • Which project stages you have supported.
  • How you coordinate with architects, engineers and other teams.
  • How you manage drawing revisions and deadlines.

3. Review Your CV Thoroughly

Employers often ask questions based on the information in your CV. Read your CV before the interview so you can confidently explain your employment history, responsibilities, qualifications and achievements.

Pay particular attention to:

  • Your previous companies and job titles.
  • Employment dates and project durations.
  • Your main responsibilities in each position.
  • Important projects and achievements.
  • Technical and professional skills.
  • Education and professional certifications.

Never include information that you cannot explain or support. Be honest about your responsibilities and experience.

4. Prepare Your Answer to "Tell Me About Yourself"

This is one of the most common interview questions. Employers usually want a short introduction to your professional background, relevant experience and career direction.

A useful answer normally includes:

  1. Your professional background.
  2. Your relevant experience and main skills.
  3. Your current or most recent responsibilities.
  4. Why you are interested in the position.
Example answer for an experienced professional:

"I am an architectural draftsman with extensive experience in architectural and construction drawing preparation. I have worked on different projects and have experience using AutoCAD and Revit. My responsibilities have included preparing architectural drawings, updating revisions and coordinating with engineering teams. I am now looking for an opportunity where I can use my experience and continue developing my professional skills."

Adjust this example to match your actual experience. Avoid memorising a long speech. Practise speaking naturally.

5. Practise Common Job Interview Questions

Preparing answers to frequently asked questions can reduce nervousness and help you communicate more clearly.

Question 1: Why do you want to work for our company?

How to answer: Explain what interests you about the company, its projects or the position. Connect that interest with your professional goals.

Example:

"I am interested in this opportunity because the position matches my experience and professional skills. I would like to contribute to your team and develop further through the projects handled by your company."

Question 2: What are your strengths?

How to answer: Mention strengths that are relevant to the job and support them with real examples.

Example:

"My strengths include attention to detail, technical drawing skills, time management and the ability to coordinate with different teams."

Question 3: What is your weakness?

How to answer: Choose a genuine area you are working to improve. Explain what you are doing to develop.

Example:

"I sometimes spend additional time checking drawing details because I want to maintain accuracy. I am improving my time management so I can balance quality and deadlines effectively."

Question 4: Why should we hire you?

How to answer: Connect your relevant experience, skills and professional attitude to the employer's requirements.

Example:

"My experience and technical skills match the requirements of this position. I understand the importance of accuracy, coordination and meeting project deadlines, and I believe I can make a positive contribution to your team."

Question 5: What are your salary expectations?

How to answer: Research typical salaries for similar roles and consider your experience, location and the responsibilities involved.

Example:

"I am open to discussing a fair salary based on the responsibilities of the position, my experience and the company's compensation structure."

6. Prepare Examples from Your Work Experience

Employers may ask you to describe a difficult situation, a problem you solved or an important contribution you made.

Use real examples. One useful approach is the STAR method:

  • Situation: Explain the situation or challenge.
  • Task: Describe your responsibility.
  • Action: Explain what you did.
  • Result: Describe the outcome.
Example:

"During a project, several architectural drawing revisions were required within a short period. I was responsible for updating the drawings and coordinating the changes with the relevant team members. I organised the revisions, checked the updated information and completed the drawings within the required timeframe."

Use examples that accurately reflect your own work. Do not exaggerate achievements or claim responsibility for work completed by someone else.

7. Prepare for Technical Interview Questions

For technical positions, employers may test your practical knowledge and ask questions about software, tools, standards, procedures and project experience.

For an architectural drafting or design position, possible topics include:

  • AutoCAD commands, layers, blocks and drawing organisation.
  • Revit Architecture modelling and documentation.
  • Architectural plans, sections and elevations.
  • Drawing scales, dimensions and annotations.
  • Construction drawing coordination.
  • Drawing revisions and quality checking.
  • Understanding project specifications and standards.
  • Coordination with structural and MEP disciplines.

Review the technical requirements in the job advertisement and practise explaining how you use the relevant tools in your daily work.

8. Prepare Your Documents

Before attending an interview, organise the documents that may be requested by the employer.

  • Updated CV or resume.
  • Copies of relevant educational certificates.
  • Professional training certificates.
  • Experience certificates, where appropriate.
  • Portfolio or examples of work, if requested and permitted.
  • Any other documents specifically requested by the employer.

Keep digital copies available on your phone or laptop. Do not share confidential company drawings, client information or restricted project documents in your portfolio.

9. Dress Professionally and Arrive on Time

Your appearance and punctuality contribute to the overall impression you make during an interview.

  • Choose clean, professional clothing suitable for the company and role.
  • Check the interview location and travel arrangements in advance.
  • Plan to arrive around 10 to 15 minutes early.
  • Keep your phone silent during the interview.
  • Greet the interviewer politely and maintain a professional attitude.

For an online interview, test your internet connection, microphone and camera beforehand. Choose a quiet place with suitable lighting.

10. Prepare Questions to Ask the Interviewer

An interview is also an opportunity for you to understand the job and the company. Prepare a few relevant questions.

For example:

  • What are the main responsibilities of this position?
  • Which projects or teams would I be working with?
  • What software and tools does the team use?
  • What are the company's expectations during the first few months?
  • What are the next steps in the recruitment process?

Ask questions that help you understand the role. Avoid asking questions that are already clearly answered in the job advertisement.

11. How to Handle Interview Nervousness

Feeling nervous before an interview is normal. Preparation and practice can help you manage it.

  • Practise answering questions aloud.
  • Prepare a short professional introduction.
  • Take a moment to think before answering difficult questions.
  • Speak clearly and avoid rushing.
  • If you do not understand a question, politely ask the interviewer to repeat it.
  • If you do not know an answer, be honest rather than guessing.
Remember: An interview is a professional conversation, not a test of whether you can give a perfect answer to every question.

12. What to Do After the Interview

Your professional approach should continue after the interview ends.

  • Thank the interviewer for their time.
  • If appropriate, send a short and polite follow-up message.
  • Note any questions you found difficult so you can prepare better next time.
  • Continue applying for suitable opportunities rather than assuming an offer is guaranteed.

13. Common Interview Mistakes to Avoid

Even well-qualified candidates can make avoidable mistakes. Try to avoid the following:

  • Arriving late without informing the interviewer.
  • Not researching the company.
  • Giving unclear or excessively long answers.
  • Providing inaccurate information about experience.
  • Speaking negatively about previous employers.
  • Interrupting the interviewer.
  • Using your phone during the interview.
  • Not understanding the job description.
  • Failing to prepare for technical questions.
  • Asking no questions about the position.

14. Job Interview Preparation Checklist

Use this checklist before your next interview:

  • Research the company.
  • Read the job description carefully.
  • Review your CV and employment history.
  • Prepare your professional introduction.
  • Practise common interview questions.
  • Prepare examples from your experience.
  • Review relevant technical skills.
  • Organise requested documents.
  • Choose suitable professional clothing.
  • Confirm the interview time and location.
  • Prepare questions for the interviewer.
  • Plan your journey or test your online interview setup.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. How many days should I prepare for a job interview?

There is no fixed number of days. If possible, begin preparing as soon as you receive the invitation. Even a few focused hours of research and practice can help.

2. What should I say when an interviewer asks me to introduce myself?

Give a brief summary of your professional background, relevant experience, important skills and interest in the position. Keep your answer focused on the job.

3. Should I memorise interview answers?

It is better to understand and practise your main points rather than memorising every sentence. Natural and honest answers are usually easier to communicate.

4. What should I bring to an interview?

Bring an updated CV and any documents requested by the employer. Depending on the position, you may also need certificates or a professional portfolio.

5. How should I answer a question if I do not know the answer?

Be honest. Explain what you do know and, if appropriate, how you would approach learning or solving the problem.

6. Should I follow up after an interview?

A short, polite thank-you message can be appropriate. If the employer provides a specific timeline, allow that time before requesting an update.

7. Can I use the same CV for every job interview?

You can use the same accurate employment history, but your CV should be tailored to each relevant position. Highlight the skills and experience that match the job requirements.

Conclusion

Preparing for a job interview involves more than practising answers. Research the company, understand the position, review your CV, prepare relevant examples and organise your documents before the interview.

Be honest about your experience, communicate clearly and approach the conversation professionally. Every interview can also help you learn and improve for future opportunities.

A well-prepared CV is an important starting point. Make sure your CV clearly presents your experience, qualifications and skills before applying for your next opportunity.
