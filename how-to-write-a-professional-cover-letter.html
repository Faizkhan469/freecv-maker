How to Write a Professional Cover Letter in 2026

Last updated: October 31, 2026

A professional cover letter is a short document that introduces you to an employer and explains why you are applying for a particular position. It gives you an opportunity to connect your experience, qualifications and skills with the requirements of a job.

While your CV provides details about your career history, a cover letter allows you to explain why your background is relevant to the opportunity.

Whether you are applying for your first job, changing careers or seeking a senior position, a clear and well-written cover letter can support your application.

1. Understand the Purpose of a Cover Letter

A cover letter should complement your CV rather than repeat every detail in it.

Its main purposes are to:

  • Introduce you to the employer.
  • Identify the position you are applying for.
  • Highlight relevant experience and qualifications.
  • Explain your interest in the role.
  • Show how your skills relate to the employer's needs.
  • Encourage the recruiter to review your CV.

A good cover letter is focused on the vacancy. It should not be a long description of your entire professional history.

2. Start with Professional Contact Details

At the top of your cover letter, include your basic contact information.

  • Full name
  • Professional email address
  • Telephone number
  • City and country
  • Date of application

You may also include the employer's name, company and address if those details are available and relevant.

Use the same contact information as your CV so that the employer can easily identify your application.

3. Use a Professional Greeting

If you know the recruiter's name, address the letter to that person. Check the spelling and use an appropriate professional greeting.

If the name is not available, you can use a general greeting such as:

  • Dear Hiring Manager,
  • Dear Recruitment Team,
  • Dear Hiring Team,

Avoid guessing the name of the person responsible for recruitment.

4. Write a Strong Opening Paragraph

Your opening paragraph should explain which position you are applying for and briefly introduce your professional background.

Try to make the first few sentences specific rather than using a generic introduction.

Example: Experienced Professional

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to apply for the Project Coordinator position advertised by your company. I have more than eight years of experience supporting project operations, coordinating documentation and working with multidisciplinary teams. I am interested in bringing my organizational and communication skills to your organization.

5. Highlight Relevant Skills and Experience

The main section of your cover letter should explain why your experience is relevant to the position.

Choose two or three important qualifications or strengths that match the job description.

For example, you might highlight:

  • Relevant years of professional experience.
  • Technical software and specialist knowledge.
  • Experience with similar projects or industries.
  • Communication and teamwork.
  • Problem-solving and organizational skills.
  • Genuine professional achievements.

Use specific examples where possible. Avoid making unsupported claims or listing every skill from your CV.

6. Explain Why You Are Interested in the Position

Employers want to understand why you are interested in their vacancy.

You can briefly explain how the role matches your experience, professional interests or career direction.

Research the organization before applying. If you know something relevant about its projects, services or industry, you may mention it naturally.

Avoid copying generic company descriptions or making statements that could apply to any employer.

7. Show What You Can Contribute

A strong cover letter focuses on how your experience may benefit the employer.

Instead of simply saying that you are hardworking, explain the relevant work you have performed and the skills you can bring to the position.

Example

In my previous role, I supported the preparation of technical documentation, coordinated information between project teams and helped maintain accurate drawing records. I believe this experience would allow me to contribute effectively to your project delivery activities.

Keep your statements truthful and relevant to the vacancy.

8. Write a Professional Closing Paragraph

End your cover letter politely and confidently.

You can:

  • Express your interest in discussing the position.
  • Thank the employer for considering your application.
  • Mention that your CV is attached.
  • Close with a professional sign-off.
Example Closing

Thank you for considering my application. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how my experience and skills could contribute to your team. Please find my CV attached for your review.

Kind regards,
Your Full Name

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am writing to express my interest in the Architectural Draftsman position at your organization. I have 14 years of experience in architectural drafting and construction documentation, including professional experience in Saudi Arabia.

My professional background includes preparing architectural drawings, updating construction documentation and supporting design coordination. I have practical experience using AutoCAD and Revit Architecture and working with project teams to maintain accurate technical information.

This is an example only. Replace the sample details with your actual experience, qualifications and the requirements of the vacancy.

10. Keep Your Cover Letter Short and Clear

Most job application cover letters should be concise. One page is generally sufficient.

Use short paragraphs and straightforward language. Avoid repeating your complete CV or adding unnecessary personal information.

Recruiters may review many applications, so make your most relevant information easy to identify.

11. Customize Your Cover Letter for Each Job

A customized cover letter is usually more useful than sending exactly the same letter to every employer.

Before submitting your application:

  • Check the job title and company name.
  • Review the job description.
  • Identify the most important required skills.
  • Highlight your matching experience.
  • Adjust your opening and closing paragraphs.
  • Remove information that is unrelated to the vacancy.

Make sure the information in your cover letter agrees with your CV.

12. Common Cover Letter Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using the wrong company name or job title.
  • Sending a generic letter without customization.
  • Repeating the entire CV.
  • Writing long paragraphs that are difficult to read.
  • Making unsupported claims about achievements.
  • Including spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • Using an unprofessional email address.
  • Forgetting to attach the CV when requested.
  • Using an overly casual or demanding tone.
  • Including unnecessary private information.

13. Cover Letter Checklist Before Sending

  • My name and contact details are correct.
  • The company name and job title are accurate.
  • I have used a professional greeting.
  • My opening paragraph clearly identifies the position.
  • I have highlighted relevant skills and experience.
  • I have explained my interest in the role.
  • My statements are accurate and honest.
  • The letter is clear and concise.
  • I have checked spelling and grammar.
  • I have followed the employer's application instructions.
  • My CV is attached if required.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a CV and a cover letter?

A CV summarizes your professional experience, education and skills. A cover letter introduces your application and explains why your background is relevant to a specific position.

Is a cover letter necessary for every job application?

Not every employer requires one. Follow the instructions in the job advertisement. If a cover letter is requested, prepare one that is relevant to the vacancy.

How long should a professional cover letter be?

One page is generally sufficient. Focus on your most relevant qualifications and keep the writing concise.

Should I use the same cover letter for every job?

It is better to customize your cover letter for each vacancy. Adjust the job title, company name and relevant skills to match the opportunity.

Can I write a cover letter without knowing the recruiter's name?

Yes. You can use a professional greeting such as Dear Hiring Manager or Dear Recruitment Team.

Should I mention salary expectations in a cover letter?

Only include salary expectations if the employer requests them or if they are specifically relevant to the application.
